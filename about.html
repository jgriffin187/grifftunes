Welcome to GriffTunes

GriffTunes v is a TouchTunes-style jukebox built for the Griffin family basement. Guests drop in coins, browse and search all of Spotify, line up songs and watch them play on a big touchscreen. The lights change with every song, and guests can join from their phones by scanning a QR code. It also has radio stations, chart countdowns, a "Grad Year" time machine, a Christmas Mode, an Edmund Fitzgerald memorial, a radio-style host called DJ Griff and more.

Josh Griffin designed it and built it with Claude, Anthropic's AI assistant. Version 1.0 was finished on October 31, 2026.

The basement setup

  • Jukebox: an ASUS Chromebox running GriffTunes full screen on a 27" touchscreen. GriffTunes is a web page hosted free on GitHub Pages (jgriffin187.github.io/grifftunes) and can be installed as an app.
  • Music: Spotify Premium. GriffTunes plays through Spotify, either on the Chromebox itself or on any Spotify Connect speaker.
  • Speakers: a WiiM Pro streamer feeding two Electro-Voice Evolve 70 column speakers. The WiiM plugs into the left Evolve, and its Mix Out sends the signal to the right Evolve's Input 1 over XLR.
  • Speaker power: two Philips Hue smart plugs named Evolve Left and Evolve Right, in a Hue room called Speakers. GriffTunes turns them on when music starts and off after a quiet stretch.
  • Lights: Philips Hue, controlled directly through the Hue Bridge on the home network (at 192.168.4.75).
  • Mirrorball: on a Hue smart plug named Mirrorball. The 🪩 button next to the lights (and on the Couch Remote) turns it on and off. Christmas Mode, the Edmund Fitzgerald memorial and Yacht Mode turn it on, and it goes off when the last of them ends. Nothing else turns it off. A different plug can be picked in Settings → Lights.
  • Phones: guests scan the QR code to search, queue songs, insert coins, change the volume and run countdowns or the radio from their phones.
  • DJ Griff: his lines are written by Google Gemini and spoken by Google Cloud Text-to-Speech; on the WiiM, his clips are passed through Firebase Storage (see DJ Griff).

Playing music

Credits and coins

  • Tap Insert coin to add credits (10 per coin by default; the amount can be changed in Settings).
  • Each song row has three buttons: + Queue (1 credit) adds it to the end of the line, Play next (2) jumps it to the front, and Play now (3) plays it right away.
  • Queue the whole album (Greatest Albums and the Christmas tab) or Queue the whole playlist (any playlist, up to its first 50 songs) costs 5 credits.
  • Free play in Settings turns credits off entirely.

Browsing

  • My Albums / My Playlists: your Spotify library, including Liked Songs.
  • Family: playlists from up to four family members' own Spotify accounts (added in Settings).
  • Artists: your 30 most-played artists plus every artist you follow on Spotify. Chips across the top switch between Most Played, Pop, Country, Hip Hop, Rock, Other and All (followed artists are A to Z). Artists you follow later show up on their own within a day, or right away with the ↻ Sync chip. If an artist lands in the wrong genre, open them and change Genre at the top of their page. Each artist page shows a row of their albums and a Greatest Hits list. Tap an album to open it, and use the "‹ Artist" button to go back.
  • 🎲 Shuffle: on My Albums, My Playlists, Artists and Christmas. The cover flow spins, ticks and slows like a prize wheel, the lights chase colors, and the winner flips forward with confetti and starts playing. An artist plays their Greatest Hits (#1 now, the rest after songs people already paid for), an album plays in order and a playlist plays 10 songs shuffled. On Artists it stays inside the genre chip you're on. Tap the covers mid-spin to skip to the result. When those songs are done, the radio or house mix takes over. It's free, and the Couch Remote has the button too (the spin happens on the big screen).
  • New Releases: recent albums from the artists you listen to, plus what's new on Spotify. Spotify releases new music on Fridays, so the list is rebuilt once a week on Friday morning and kept all week, which makes it open instantly.
  • Greatest Albums: Rolling Stone's top 50 albums of all time, playable one song at a time or as a whole album.
  • Countdown and Grad Year are described below.
  • 🎄 Christmas: appears to the right of Grad Year while the Christmas tree setting is on. About 220 Christmas albums (up to 3 per artist), in a new random order each time you open the tab, with chips for Classics, Pop, Country, R&B & Soul, Christian, Instrumental, Kids & Movies and Your Christmas (your own playlists with Christmas or holiday in the name, always shown first). The first card starts the Christmas Countdown (the 25 biggest Christmas hits). Albums can be played one song at a time or queued whole. Its Shuffle is a Snowy Shuffle, with sleigh-bell ticks, red and green lights and snowflakes.
  • Search: the search bar finds songs, albums and artists. The on-screen keyboard is for the touchscreen. If a microphone is connected, a mic button lets you search by voice.

Now Playing

  • Play/pause, skip, volume, speaker picker, radio, lyrics and the full-screen turntable.
  • Tap the album art to open that album and pick another song from it.
  • The colored tag in the corner says what's playing: house mix, a radio station, a countdown position, the class year, Christmas Mode, or FROM · an album or playlist that's carrying on.
  • Up Next shows the line of paid picks. The Clear button at the top (tap it twice) removes them all; it's on the touchscreen and the Couch Remote, not guests' phones. When it's empty, a running countdown or Christmas Mode keeps going first. Otherwise the album, playlist or artist's hits list the last picked song came from carries on in order (a song from search continues with the rest of its album), with a FROM · tag in Now Playing. When that runs out, the radio or the house mix takes over. These songs are free.

Speakers

  • The speaker button lists Spotify Connect speakers on the account (WiiM, TVs, Echo, Sonos and so on). Pick one and the music moves there.
  • If the WiiM doesn't appear, play something to it once from the Spotify app on a phone, then tap Refresh. Spotify only lists speakers that are awake. A speaker shown as just "Speaker" is usually the WiiM (Spotify sends it a code instead of a name).

Radio

On the jukebox, the radio button in Now Playing opens the Alpine car radio (below): tap a numbered preset to pick a station. It starts right away, and songs people pay for still play first; while one of those plays, the radio button stays lit to show the station comes back afterward. On phones, the radio button turns the radio on and off and the FM dial picks the station.

  • My Library / My Artists: your liked songs, or hits from your top and followed artists.
  • 60s, 70s, 80s, 90s, 2000s, Today: popular songs from each era.
  • Country, Classic Rock, Yacht Rock, Rock, Pop, Hip-Hop, R&B, Alternative, Dance, Christian Hits: each plays from a hand-picked list of about 30 artists, some limited to their era. Spotify's own genre tags let in too many unrelated songs. Each refill takes at most 3 songs from any one artist, and the station won't repeat an artist within 4 songs when there's another choice.

Each artist's songs are remembered for three days, so stations rarely need to ask Spotify for anything.

The Alpine car radio: on the jukebox the radio is a faceplate modeled on Josh's own 1990s Alpine 7280 cassette deck. The 📻 button (or the green LCD in Now Playing) opens it big across the screen:

  • Presets 1–6 pick a station. BAND flips between FM1 (60s to Today), FM2 (Country, Classic Rock, Yacht Rock, Rock, Pop, Hip-Hop) and FM3 (R&B, Alternative, Dance, Christian Hits, My Library, My Artists). The playing station's button glows white with a red dot.
  • DN / UP and SEEK step through every station in order of frequency; the LCD rolls through the frequencies like a real radio seeking.
  • The knob with the green ring is the volume (drag it around or up and down); tapping it turns the radio off. ▶▶ skips the song, ⏏ or Close puts the faceplate away (it also closes itself after 30 seconds).
  • The LCD shows the band, the frequency and the station, and every few seconds scrolls the song and artist like an RDS radio. MONO, LOUD and DX just light their indicators, for fun.
  • Phones keep the tuning dial, which fits a phone screen.

🎙 DJ Griff

A radio-style host who comes on every 3–5 songs to introduce the next one, sometimes with a fun fact about the artist or the song's chart run. He knows when a song was requested, which radio station is on, where a countdown is, when an album or playlist is carrying on and when it's Christmas. He only comes on after a song ends by itself, never after a skip. The latest line also shows on screen.

  • How it sounds: he follows the music. On the WiiM, the music pauses for a few seconds while he talks, then the next song starts (the WiiM plays one source at a time). On this jukebox, for example with a Bluetooth speaker connected to the Chromebox, the next song starts quietly and he talks over its intro, then the music comes back up. On any other Spotify speaker he stays quiet.
  • How it works: Google Gemini writes the line from facts GriffTunes already has (it's told never to make up facts), and Google Cloud Text-to-Speech speaks it. On the WiiM, GriffTunes uploads the clip to Firebase Storage and tells the WiiM over Wi-Fi to play that link, then starts the next song on Spotify. On this jukebox the clip plays straight from the Chromebox. The next intro is prepared while the current song plays.
  • Voices: 30 Google Chirp 3 HD voices (16 male, 14 female), each in American, British or Australian English, with five favorites at the top. ▶ Preview DJ Griff plays a sample in the chosen voice.
  • Pause before he talks: each clip starts with real silence (1.5 seconds unless you change it) so the WiiM and the speakers are fully awake before his first word.
  • Setup: Settings → Extras → DJ Griff. Paste a Google Cloud key for the voice and a Google AI Studio key for the writing, pick a voice, enter the WiiM's address (192.168.4.108) and the Firebase bucket (grifftunes-dj.firebasestorage.app), tap Trust the WiiM once, then Test on the WiiM, then turn him on. Preview DJ Griff makes a sample any time. Firebase deletes clips after a day. The keys and settings travel in the setup file.
  • Voices: five Google voices to choose from: Griff · upbeat (the default), Deep & smooth, Big energy, Bright and Warm.
  • If something fails (no key, no network, Google says no), that break is skipped and the music carries on; after two failures in a row a message says why. Skipping during a break ends it, and a song someone starts during a break wins.
  • On the WiiM: GriffTunes waits for the clip plus a few seconds, then waits for Spotify to see the WiiM again before starting the next song, so the music stays on the Evolves.

🎙 Ask DJ Griff

Tap Ask DJ at the top (on the touchscreen when a microphone is connected, and on guests' phones) and say a song, like "Purple Rain by Prince". Phones can also type it.

  • Understanding: the phone or the jukebox turns the words into text, and Google Gemini (DJ Griff's Writing key) works out the title and artist, fixing words misheard over loud music.
  • Finding it: the song library first (no Spotify search), then one gentle Spotify search only if the library doesn't have it. While Spotify search is blocked, or after 12 voice searches in an hour, it sticks to the library.
  • Queue, next or now: works like the three buttons, picked by what you say. "Play X" adds it to the end of Up Next (1 credit), "play X next" puts it at the front (2), "play X now" stops the current song and plays it (3). When the music is paused or stopped, or only the radio, house mix or a countdown is on, it starts right away for 1 credit. A paid song that gets cut off, or was paused, goes back to the front of Up Next. Albums are 5 credits either way. The couch remote and Free play cost nothing. A song already playing or in line isn't added twice ("next" or "now" moves it up).
  • The answer: on screen, and DJ Griff says it out loud in his voice and personality. On the WiiM the song pauses for a few seconds while he talks, then picks up where it was; when the song would start right away, he talks first. Turn his voice off in Settings → DJ Griff.
  • Whole albums: "Hey DJ, play the Alanis Morissette Jagged Little Pill album" queues the whole album for 5 credits, the same as Queue the whole album. It's found in My Albums, the Christmas albums or the artist's own album list first (from a song in the library or the Artists tab), and only then with one Spotify album search.
  • Not found: it says so and shows close matches to tap, such as other songs by that artist in the library. Saying only an artist ("play some Prince") shows a few of their songs to pick from.
  • "Hey DJ": with Settings → DJ Griff → Listen for "Hey DJ" on, just say "Hey DJ, play Purple Rain by Prince" from across the room. The jukebox listens for the wake word on its own microphone, on the Chromebox itself: nothing is sent anywhere until it hears "Hey DJ". Then the music dips for a few seconds while it records the request, and that short clip goes to Gemini to be understood (without a Writing key, the browser's speech recognition takes over instead). Sensitivity trades missed requests against false alarms; while Settings is open, a meter shows what it hears so you can test it. A short pause after "Hey DJ" helps it catch the wake word.
  • "Hey DJ, roast me": about 30 seconds of DJ Griff making fun of your music taste, in his personality setting and free. From the jukebox mic or the couch remote, "me" is the Spotify account's top and followed artists, saved albums and playlist names, plus what the jukebox played in the last few hours. "Roast Liz" uses that family member's linked playlists. "Roast the room", or "roast me" from a guest's phone, uses only what's been played lately. The artists, songs and playlists are real; the jokes are made up.
  • "Hey DJ, it's beginning to look a lot like Christmas": stops whatever is playing, turns on Christmas Mode (and the Christmas tree setting, if it was off) and starts a Christmas song right away, with DJ Griff kicking off the season. A paid song that gets cut off goes back to the front of Up Next. "Turn on Christmas mode" and "let's get festive" work too; "Christmas is over" or "turn off Christmas mode" turns it off. ("Play It's Beginning to Look a Lot Like Christmas" still just plays that song.)
  • "Hey DJ, the gales of November came early": opens the Edmund Fitzgerald memorial and starts "The Wreck of the Edmund Fitzgerald" right away, exactly like tapping the ship's bell (a paid song that's cut off goes back to the front of Up Next). DJ Griff stays quiet for this one. "Play The Wreck of the Edmund Fitzgerald" still just plays the song.
  • Controls: "next track" / "skip this song", "pause", "unpause" / "resume", "turn it up" / "turn it down" (5 steps; "a lot" is 15), "volume to 40", "mute" / "unmute", "clear the queue" (right away, no undo), "play the 80s station" (any station) / "radio off", "lights on" / "lights off", "speakers on" / "speakers off", "turn the mirrorball on" / "mirrorball off". They work at the jukebox (Hey DJ or Ask DJ) and from the couch remote, quietly with a short note on screen. Guests' phones get "Only the couch remote can do that", like the buttons. "What's playing?" / "who sings this?" gets an answer from DJ Griff out loud, sometimes with a fact, from anywhere.
  • Made-to-order stations: "play some 90s country", "80s pop", "90s rap", "current hits", "70s disco", "yacht rock". Gemini picks the years and 25 to 40 fitting artists; the station plays library hits by them from those years plus their Spotify top songs from the same years (no searches). It starts right away, free, and plays like any radio station (it shows on the Alpine radio) until you change stations or say "radio off". If the library has fewer than 15 matching songs, the years widen by 5 each way; under 5, it suggests the closest built-in station.

Countdowns

  • Hot 100 Rewind: the Billboard Hot 100 Top 20 from last week, or from this same week 1, 5, 10 … 40 years ago, counted down from #20 to #1.
  • Country Gold: a year's biggest #1 country hits.
  • Christmas Countdown: (when the Christmas tree setting is on) the 25 biggest Christmas songs in Hot 100 Top 20 history. Start it from the first card on the 🎄 Christmas tab.
  • Press Start countdown, or tap any song to start there. The countdown keeps going toward #1.
  • Each song gets a pop-up card with its chart stats. Every fourth song, a "Flashback" card shows a few facts from that year.
  • Songs that aren't on Spotify are skipped.

Grad Year

Pick a graduation year (1959 to today) to hear the 40 biggest songs of that senior year, September through mid-June. Songs are ranked by how high and how long they charted in the Hot 100 Top 20.

The fun-fact ticker

During a countdown, Grad Year or Christmas Mode, a big ticker scrolls along the bottom of the screen, under the albums, in large type. It also appears, a bit smaller, in the screensaver mini player and on the full-screen turntable. On phones it stays in Now Playing.

  • Artist facts first: a line or two from Wikipedia, the artist's age when the song charted, their Hot 100 Top 20 record and how many songs they have in the countdown.
  • Then the year: "Meanwhile…" events from around the time the song charted, plus the year's #1 TV show, Best Picture, top movie, Super Bowl and World Series winners, Time Person of the Year, the President, and prices for gas, stamps and the minimum wage.

Lyrics, turntable and screensavers

  • Lyrics: time-synced lyrics scroll along with the song when they're available.
  • Turntable: a full-screen video turntable, with the album art on the spinning record label, the song caption, Up Next, controls and optional lyrics.
  • Screensaver: starts after the jukebox sits untouched (90 seconds by default). Choose:
    • Fine art gallery: public-domain paintings from the Art Institute of Chicago, with a mini player that has play/pause and skip buttons.
    • Now playing: big album art.
    • Turntable
    80s and 90s stations: while the radio is on the 80s station, the screensaver is a silent, looping 80s concert video; on the 90s station, a retro weather-channel screen. Both show the mini player and replace whichever screensaver is picked. Christmas, the Fitzgerald memorial and Yacht Mode come first. Turn them off in Settings → Screen → Decade video screensavers. Touching the screen anywhere else ends the screensaver.

Christmas Mode

Turn on Show the Christmas tree button in Settings. Then tap the tree in the top bar, which also counts the days until Christmas.

  • Music: the built-in Christmas Top 40 (40 classic Christmas songs, shuffled), or your own Spotify playlist (chosen in Settings). Paid picks still play first.
  • Lights: red and green only (no white), at about 70% of your normal brightness. Every 30 seconds one bulb slowly fades to another red or green, so the room keeps changing gently. White-only bulbs drop to a dim candle glow.
  • On screen: snow, twinkling string lights around the edges, and Santa's sleigh flying across once per song with sleigh bells.
  • ❄ Frosty screen: while the Christmas look is on and nobody touches the jukebox for a minute, frost creeps in from the edges like a window freezing over, thick at the edges with a see-through haze in the middle (fully frosted after about 8 minutes). Drag a finger to wipe a clear streak; it frosts back over in about a minute. Wiping over the volume slider won't change the volume: volume sliders only move when you drag the knob itself, so a stray swipe or a tap on the bar can't jump the volume. When frost builds up, a 🔥 Heater button appears in the bottom corner: tap it to melt the ice from the bottom up, with drips. Two minutes after the screen is fully frosted, the heater comes on by itself. Turn it off in Settings → Extras → Frosty screen. Not on phones.
  • Christmas tab: a 🎄 Christmas tab of about 220 Christmas albums, your Christmas playlists and the Christmas Countdown (see Browsing). Opening it turns on the Christmas look (snow, string lights, Santa's sleigh, Christmas Hue colors and the fireplace screensaver) without changing the music; leaving it turns the look back off unless Christmas Mode is on.
  • Ticker: a candy-cane stripe with Christmas trivia.
  • Coins: they turn into gift boxes.
  • Screensaver: a real crackling fireplace video.

⛵ Yacht Mode

Comes on by itself while the Yacht Rock station plays (songs people pay for in between keep it on). The sailboat button in the top bar, or "Hey DJ, set sail", turns it on and starts the Yacht Rock station; tap it again, or say "Hey DJ, dock the boat", to turn both off. Christmas Mode and the Edmund Fitzgerald memorial take priority.

  • On screen: a golden-hour sea behind everything, a sailboat that glides along the bottom once per song, a varnished teak frame with a rope rail, signal flags strung across the top, a captain's hat on the coin and sand dollars for coins.
  • Extras: warm string lights twinkle between the flags; every so often a gust of wind ripples down the flag line; the water glints where the sun hits it; the sea rocks gently like you're on a boat (turn that off in Settings → Fun); seagulls fly past, and now and then one lands on the bottom rope, hops around and takes off again; and the Now Playing cover sits behind a brass porthole.
  • The flags follow the Smooth-o-Meter: a Certified Yacht song gets a fresh breeze (the flags snap and the lights twinkle faster), and a Dinghy leaves them hanging limp with the lights dimmed.
  • Smooth-o-Meter: in Now Playing. A song by a Yacht Rock station artist is Certified Yacht; a hit from 1976 to 1984 is Yacht-adjacent; anything else is a Dinghy.
  • Lights: sunset orange, coral, gold, teal and sea blue at about 70%, one bulb drifting to a new color every 30 seconds.
  • Screensaver: a sunset over rolling waves on a beach, looping without a seam, with the mini player.
  • Ticker: the usual artist and year facts under a blue-and-white YACHT badge.
  • DJ Griff becomes Captain Griff, a smooth late-night yacht rock host, at your personality level.
  • Phones get the sunset colors and the Smooth-o-Meter. Turn the button off in Settings → Fun.

🔔 Edmund Fitzgerald memorial

Tap the ship's bell (Fitz) next to the Christmas tree in the top bar. Gordon Lightfoot's "The Wreck of the Edmund Fitzgerald" starts right away, ahead of Up Next (a paid song that was playing goes back to the front of the line), and the screen becomes a full-screen memorial. The only music controls are the mini player at the bottom.

  • The voyage: a nautical map of Lake Superior where the Fitzgerald, with the Arthur M. Anderson behind her, sails from Superior, Wisconsin, on November 9, 1975, in step with the song. The clock, wind and waves follow the record. Under each card a side view shows the Fitzgerald with the waves drawn to the same height scale: her deck sat 11½ feet above the water when she left, and by evening the waves roll over it. She pitches with the sea, rides lower after the 3:30 PM damage report, and goes under at 7:15; a wind arrow swings from northeast to northwest when the storm passes Marquette. the screen fills with sleet as the storm builds, and 16 story cards cover the gale warnings, the storm passing Marquette, Six Fathom Shoal, the lost radars, "We are holding our own," the sinking, the search and the bell, each with a photo (the ship in 1971, the Anderson arriving in Duluth days after the storm, the National Weather Service's wind and wave models of that evening, the weather map of November 10, 1975, the NTSB chart of both ships' tracks, a recovered lifeboat, an underwater shot of her name on the pilothouse, and the Fitzgerald's own bell). The course is approximate.
  • Explore: tap the story or the map to stop and look around: tap places on the map, or read about the ship, all 29 crew, the storm, why she sank (the main theories, none proven), the bell, the song and the sources. Resume voyage picks the story back up.
  • Ring the bell: 29 tolls about six seconds apart, over a dim photo of the Fitzgerald's real bell, each showing one crew member's name, job, age and hometown and lighting a candle, with the Hue lights flashing white. A 30th toll is for all mariners lost on the Great Lakes, as at the Great Lakes Shipwreck Museum and Mariners' Church each November 10. The bell plays on the WiiM when the music is there (using the WiiM address from DJ Griff's settings), otherwise on the jukebox.
  • Mood: the lights go a dim stormy blue while the memorial is open (lights that are off stay off); Christmas effects, frost and the screensaver pause. When the song ends the music waits and offers the bell. Close goes back to the jukebox and Up Next; the memorial also closes by itself 3 minutes after the song or the bell finishes.
  • Ready ahead of time: a minute after GriffTunes starts, it quietly loads the map code, the bell and the album cover, and the jukebox keeps them (and the fonts) saved, so the memorial opens instantly and the bell starts without a pause. Only the map pictures need the internet.
  • Turn the button off in Settings → Extras. Not on phones.

🧣 All Too Well (10 Minute Version)

Tap the red scarf (10:13) in the top bar, then tap it again within 4 seconds to confirm, or say "Hey DJ, play the ten minute version". Taylor Swift's "All Too Well (10 Minute Version) (Taylor's Version)" starts right away, free, ahead of Up Next (a paid song that was playing goes back to the front of the line). The look also comes on by itself whenever the 10-minute version plays, however it was picked, and goes away when it ends.

  • The scarf: a red knit scarf knits itself around the screen, with knotted, fringed ends hanging from the top corners.
  • Autumn: maple leaves fall and pile up along the bottom. In the last chapter, snow starts falling instead.
  • Chapter cards: typewriter title cards mark the story (Autumn, Upstate, The Kitchen, Twenty-One, The Bridge, The Scarf, First Snow). The times come from the song's synced lyrics; no lyrics are shown.
  • Polaroid: the Now Playing cover becomes a tilted Polaroid that slowly fades to sepia over the ten minutes.
  • Heartbreak-o-Meter: climbs from "Mild ache" to "Devastated", and flashes SING IT for the bridge.
  • Lights: burgundy, red, amber, rust and candle gold that slowly breathe; a few seconds of cool white open-fridge light in the kitchen; full brightness for the bridge; pale winter blue at the end. The lights are never switched off, and the mirrorball stays as it is.
  • Afterwards: if the song plays to the end, a folded red scarf stays in the bottom-left corner for the rest of the night (8 hours, or until the hidden reset). Tap it.
  • Christmas Mode and the Fitzgerald memorial come first. Guests' phones can't start it. Turn the button off in Settings → Fun.

Phones

Tap the phone button or Add from phone to show a QR code. Guests' phones connect straight to the jukebox. Nothing is installed, and they don't need a Spotify account.

  • Guests can: search and browse, queue songs with the jukebox's credits, insert coins, change the volume, open albums and artists, start or stop countdowns and Grad Year, use the radio and turn Christmas Mode on or off.
  • Settings: phone requests, phone volume and phone coins can each be turned off.

Couch remote

Your own phone can be the jukebox remote. In Settings → Couch remote for your phone, scan the QR code with your phone, then use Share → Add to Home Screen so it opens like an app called Couch Remote. It plays songs for free and adds play/pause, skip, the volume slider, the speaker picker, removing songs from Up Next (one at a time, or all with Clear), the 🎲 Shuffle button, a Queue the whole album button on every album, and two big-screen switches: the turntable button opens or closes the full-screen turntable on the touchscreen, and the lyrics button turns lyrics on or off for the turntable and the screensaver. Below the volume, Speakers switches the Evolve smart plugs on or off and Lights turns the Hue lights on or off (off also tells GriffTunes to leave the lights alone until you turn them back on). Each shows only when that part of Hue is set up. Spotify search shows whether Spotify is still blocking searches (red BLOCKED, with when it started and when it was last checked), green WORKING, or orange BUSY with a countdown when Spotify has asked GriffTunes to slow down. Tap it to check right now (one search, at most every 10 minutes); while blocked, the jukebox also checks by itself every 2 hours. Reload jukebox (tap it, then tap again within 4 seconds) reloads GriffTunes on the touchscreen, for when something looks stuck: the music keeps playing on the speaker and is picked back up, Up Next is kept, and the remote reconnects by itself a few seconds later. The code is a secret link: New code on that screen makes the old one an ordinary guest phone again.

Hue lights and speaker power

  • Album colors: the room takes on the colors of each song's album art. With the setting on, radio stations can use their own colors instead.
  • Party: a bright color loop.
  • Chill: warm and dim.
  • Hands off: GriffTunes leaves the lights alone.
  • Lights on/off: the lights button opens the Lights window; its Lights row turns the room's lights on or off right away, next to the Speakers row. The light mode stays as it is, so the next song after a quiet stretch can still turn them back on.
  • Lights on with the music: when the first song starts after a quiet stretch (or after the jukebox starts up) and every light in the room is off, GriffTunes turns them on, along with the speakers. If even one light is on, it leaves them as they are. It never does this in Hands off, and you can turn it off in Settings → Philips Hue.
  • Extras: a gold flash when someone inserts a coin, and the room dims during the screensaver. Otherwise, lights that are switched off stay off.
  • Speaker plugs: plugs named like speakers are found automatically. GriffTunes turns them on when music starts, can switch playback to a chosen speaker, and turns them off after the quiet time you set.
  • If the bridge moves: if the Hue Bridge gets a new address, GriffTunes finds it again on its own.

Settings at a glance

Settings has six tabs down the left side (across the top on a phone-sized screen). It reopens on the tab you used last.

  • Spotify: jukebox name, Demo or Spotify Premium, your Spotify Client ID, and family members' playlists.
  • Jukebox: free play, house music, sounds, the on-screen keyboard, credits per coin, plus Reset credits and Clear queue.
  • Phones: what guests' phones can do, and the Couch Remote code.
  • Lights: Philips Hue lights and the speaker smart plugs.
  • Screen: the screensaver, full screen, keeping the screen awake, the weekly fresh start and installing the app.
  • Extras: Christmas Mode, the song library, Move to a new jukebox and DJ Griff (on/off, keys, voice, the WiiM address and Firebase bucket, Preview and the WiiM test).

The Save button stays at the bottom of every tab and saves changes from all tabs at once. Hue settings, the song library's overnight switch, the Christmas tree switch and everything in DJ Griff take effect the moment you change them.

Moving to a new jukebox

  1. On the old jukebox, open Settings → Extras → Save setup file. It lands in Downloads; move it to Google Drive (or a USB stick).
  2. Turn the old jukebox off and plug the touchscreen into the new one.
  3. On the new jukebox, open GriffTunes, then Settings → Extras → Load setup file (tap twice), and pick the file.
  4. GriffTunes reloads. Sign in to Spotify once and tap Agree.

The setup file carries the Spotify Client ID, the Hue Bridge pairing and speaker plugs, family members, genre picks, credits, Up Next, the Couch Remote code (so your phone keeps working) and DJ Griff's keys and settings. It holds private keys: delete it after the move. The full song library loads from GitHub on its own. For DJ Griff on the new jukebox, tap Trust the WiiM once more.

Troubleshooting

  • The badge in the top-left is orange ("Spotify busy" or "not answering"): Spotify asked GriffTunes to slow down. The badge counts down and checks again on its own, then chimes "Spotify is back." Avoid tapping a lot meanwhile. If it lasts hours, give GriffTunes a fresh Spotify Client ID (see "For developers").
  • The speaker shows as just "Speaker": Spotify sent a code instead of a name (common with the WiiM). Giving the speaker a name in its own app (WiiM Home for the WiiM) may fix it.
  • The WiiM isn't in the speaker list: play to it once from the Spotify app on a phone, then tap Refresh.
  • DJ Griff is quiet: make sure he's on and the music is on the WiiM or this jukebox (he stays quiet on other speakers). Tap Preview DJ Griff or Test on the WiiM: the message says what's wrong. If the WiiM can't be reached, tap Trust the WiiM again (Chrome sometimes forgets) and allow local network access.
  • "Not secure" at the top of the window: Chrome adds it after GriffTunes talks to the WiiM, whose certificate is homemade. Nothing is less secure. True full screen (the full-screen key) hides it.
  • Lights stopped responding: open the Lights panel. GriffTunes searches for a bridge that moved. If the bridge was reset, pair it again (press the bridge button, then Connect).
  • Something looks stuck: reload the page. The jukebox also reloads itself for updates once things are quiet, and once a week at the time set in Settings.
  • Power: keep the Chromebox from sleeping (ChromeOS Settings → Device → Power → Keep display on). Restart ChromeOS now and then to install system updates.

Where the data comes from

  • Music, artwork and your library: Spotify Web API and Web Playback SDK.
  • Hot 100 archive (1958 to now, Top 20 each week): utdata/rwd-billboard-data (MIT license), based on Billboard's charts. A GitHub job adds each new week every Wednesday.
  • Country Gold: Billboard Hot Country Songs #1s, compiled from Wikipedia's yearly lists. Country Airplay #1s fill in thin years.
  • Greatest Albums: Rolling Stone's "500 Greatest Albums of All Time" (2020 edition), ranks 1–50.
  • Year facts:
    • Wikipedia "Year in the United States" pages, for light, fun events only.
    • Wikipedia lists for TV, the box office, the Oscars, the Super Bowl, the World Series and Time Person of the Year.
    • USPS (stamps), the U.S. Department of Labor (minimum wage), and the Department of Energy and FRED (gas).
  • Artist facts: Wikipedia (summary) and Wikidata (birth dates), looked up live and remembered for 30 days.
  • Lyrics: LRCLIB, a free, open lyrics database.
  • "Hey DJ": openWakeWord (David Scripka, Apache-2.0) models run with onnxruntime-web (Microsoft, MIT license), both stored with the app; the Hey DJ model is trained with openWakeWord's tools and Piper voices.
  • DJ Griff: Google Gemini (the lines), Google Cloud Text-to-Speech (the voice) and Firebase Storage (holding clips for the WiiM for a day).
  • Edmund Fitzgerald memorial: facts from S.S. Edmund Fitzgerald Online, the Great Lakes Shipwreck Museum, the Coast Guard and NTSB reports, CIMSS and the National Weather Service in Marquette; the map is Esri's Ocean Basemap (depths from GEBCO and NOAA), drawn with Leaflet 1.9.4 (BSD license), which is stored with the app. Photos come from Wikimedia Commons (public domain, CC0 or Creative Commons licenses), plus one underwater video still of the wreck (photographer unknown) stored with the app; each one's credit is shown under it and listed on the memorial's Sources tab. The bell sound is synthesized and stored with the app.
  • Christmas albums: about 220 albums picked by hand and matched to Spotify ahead of time.
  • Paintings: Art Institute of Chicago open-access collection (public domain).
  • Phone link: PeerJS (a free connection service) and qrcode-generator.
  • Fonts: Bungee, Monoton, Outfit and IM Fell English from Google Fonts; the Alpine radio's LCD uses DSEG7 and DSEG14 Classic by keshikan (SIL Open Font License), stored with the app.
  • Videos: the turntable and fireplace loops are video files stored with the app.

For developers

How it's built

  • No build step: the whole app is one file, index.html (HTML, CSS and JavaScript, about 470 KB), with no frameworks. Editing it and pushing to main on GitHub publishes it through GitHub Pages within a minute or two. The jukebox notices the new version and reloads itself when it's quiet.
  • Other files:
    • deckvideo.js + turntable-loop.mp4: the turntable.
    • fireplace-loop.mp4: the Christmas screensaver.
    • sw.js + manifest.webmanifest: installable app and offline start.
    • hot100-top20.json, country-top20.json, grad-facts.json, greatest-albums.json: data.
    • christmas-albums.json: the Christmas tab's albums, matched to Spotify ahead of time (ID, title, artist, year, cover and chip group), so the tab needs no searches.
    • artist-genres.json: which genre chip each artist goes under (Spotify no longer gives genres to personal apps). Built from the Spotify genre tags in a public Billboard dataset (about 3,700 artists) plus hand-picked artist IDs.
    • scripts/update_charts.py + .github/workflows/update-charts.yml: the weekly chart update.
  • Providers: DEMO (made-up music, no sound), SPOTIFY and REMOTE (phones, which ask the jukebox for everything) all answer the same questions: browse a tab, list a song list, search, artist albums and so on.
  • Engines: DemoEngine and SpotifyEngine handle playback. Spotify plays in the browser through the Web Playback SDK, or on a Connect speaker through the Web API. GriffTunes checks the speaker every 30 seconds mid-song and every 3 seconds near the end, so it knows when to start the next song.
  • Spotify sign-in: PKCE (no server needed). Keys are refreshed ahead of expiry, one window at a time, and kept through network hiccups. Family accounts each get their own keys, read-only.
  • Phone link: PeerJS (WebRTC). The jukebox is the host. Phones send messages such as enqueue, album, coin, vol, cd, grad, radio, xmas, browse, tracks, search, artistAlbums and albumOf; the Couch Remote can also send play, skip, qdel, qclear, shuffle, devices, transfer, deck, lyrics and power. The jukebox broadcasts its state back.
  • Hue: the local Hue API (v1) straight from the browser to the bridge. Album colors come from sampling the cover art.
  • DJ Griff: Gemini (gemini-2.5-flash) writes from a short fact sheet, Cloud Text-to-Speech (Chirp 3 HD voices) returns an MP3, and the next intro is made about 8 seconds into the song before it. On the WiiM, the MP3 is uploaded to Firebase Storage as dj-….mp3 and the WiiM's local HTTP API is told setPlayerCmd:play:<link>. On this jukebox, the Web Playback SDK volume is lowered while the clip plays.

Living within Spotify's limits

  • Development-mode rules: GriffTunes uses a Spotify "development mode" app.
    • Up to 5 people (you plus 4) can sign in.
    • Searches return at most 10 results.
    • Only playlists you own or collaborate on can be read; Spotify-made playlists can't.
    • Rate limits are strict.
  • Request budget: at most 20 requests per 30 seconds and 500 per hour, shared by every GriffTunes window on the computer. Play, pause, skip and volume always go straight through.
  • When Spotify says "too fast": GriffTunes stops asking for 30 seconds, doubling up to 5 minutes, and remembers that wait across reloads. A hard block often looks like a network failure, so two failures in a row count as one.
  • Fresh Client ID: if a block lasts for hours, create a new app at developer.spotify.com/dashboard.
    • Redirect URI: https://jgriffin187.github.io/grifftunes/
    • Turn on Web API and Web Playback SDK.
    • Add each user under User Management.
    • Paste the new Client ID in Settings and reconnect.

What GriffTunes remembers

  • Browser storage (jbx.* keys): settings, credits, the queue, Spotify keys, Hue pairing, the Spotify block timer and request log, DJ Griff's keys, plus caches: artist and album lists (refreshed daily, kept up to a week), New Releases (a week, from Friday morning), followed artists (20 hours), radio artists (3 days), Wikipedia facts (30 days), paintings and more. If storage fills up, caches are cleared first so settings always save.
  • In memory: browse tabs (refreshed quietly after 3 minutes) and playlists (re-checked after 2 minutes).
  • Song library (IndexedDB grifftunes/songs): every chart song matched to Spotify, kept for good. It fills in overnight (1–6 AM, while idle). An optional song-library.json in the repo is loaded as a head start.
  • Countdowns start immediately: the first song to play is found first, and the rest are looked up in the background in play order.

Testing

Changes were tested with Playwright (an automated Chromium browser) against demo mode and stand-in Spotify, Wikipedia, Hue, PeerJS, Google (Gemini and Text-to-Speech), Firebase and WiiM replies. The tests covered the full screen, smaller screens and phone screens.
